Research Statement

Tick saliva carrying the disaccharide glycan galactose-alpha-1,3-galactose cause ɑ-gal B-cells to alert T-cells, which then command B-cells to produce ɑ-gal sensitive IgE antibodies through a class change, causing a temporary allergen to red meat since the disaccharide is present in red meat. Previous researchers hypothesized delayed allergic reaction times to ɑ-gal occurred due to the disaccharides attachment to glycolipids through basophil releasing only in response to digestion 5 hours after consumption. My future research plans to further prove or disprove the current theory of delayed reactions and discover the causal relationship that leads to lone star ticks carrying ɑ-gal. Understanding Alpha-Gal syndrome can lead to prevention of an allergy to glycan galactose-alpha-1,3-galactose and methods to cure it rather than waiting for IgE antibodies to die.
